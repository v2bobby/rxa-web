The gap

A supply
chain that
forgets
the patient

Every actor in the chain — manufacturer, distributor, pharmacy, regulator — holds one fragment of the truth about a pack of medicine. None of them hold the patient's fragment. That is the gap RxLoop closes.

A falsified pack looks like medicine, costs like medicine, and is sold by someone who believes it is medicine. The failure is not one bad actor — it is that nobody downstream can check, and when something goes wrong there is no route for that signal to travel back up.

The patient is the only person who touches every pack — and the only one with no way to check it.

RxLoop is not a scanner company or a serialisation vendor. It is the middleware layer between people and the medicines they already have: reference guidance they can read in their own language, a way to flag a pack that looks wrong, and reminders that make a full course actually get finished. All of it offline-first, because the places where falsified medicine circulates are the same places where data does not.

Public-interest tier

The sequencing matters: the patient app has to be genuinely useful with no partners at all, or there is nothing to bring a manufacturer to the table with. That is why the offline reference layer shipped first and the computer-vision verification has not shipped at all.
