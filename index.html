On this site

A layer between people and the medicine they hold

RxLoop gives guidance a person can read in their own language, captures a report when a pack looks wrong, and keeps a course on schedule. All of it runs offline.

The gap Why falsified medicine reaches patients, and the one person nobody equips to check it. Reference Look up fourteen common medicines in five Nigerian languages. Works with no connection. Report a pack Flag a pack that looks wrong in fifteen seconds, with no account. Reminders Set dose times that fit your day and finish the whole course. The loop How a patient’s report travels back to pharmacies, manufacturers and regulators. Product Everything the app does today, and what it does not do yet.
Business model

Free for the person holding the pack

The patient side stays free permanently. Revenue comes from the actors who gain operational value from the return path, and none of it involves selling individual health data.

Who pays, what they get, and what it costs
WhoWhat they getBasisStatus
Patients Reference guidance, offline access, reporting and reminders in five languages. Free, permanently Live
Pharmacies Batch alerts on packs their own customers flagged, plus stock and dispensing records. Per outlet, monthly Pilot stage
Manufacturers Confirm a pack against their own serialisation data. See where imitations surface first. Usage-based licence Needs partner
Regulators Anonymised clusters of suspect reports, ahead of formal reporting channels. Public-interest tier Needs backend

The sequencing matters: the patient app has to be genuinely useful with no partners at all, or there is nothing to bring a manufacturer to the table with. That is why the offline reference layer shipped first and the computer-vision verification has not shipped at all.
